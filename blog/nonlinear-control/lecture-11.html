exam review
1- contraction mapping

let \((x,\|\cdot\|)\) be a complete normed space. let \(P:x\rightarrow x\) be a contraction. then there exists a unique fixed point for your function P.

  • let \(x_n:=P(x_{n-1}),x_0\in X\) be any vector, then vector (\(x_n\)) is a Cauchy sequence that converges to the fixed point of P.
  • complete normed space: every Cauchy sequence converges to a point in the space.
  • \(\forall \epsilon >0, \exists N<\infty\) s.t. \(\forall n,m \le N, \|x_n-x_m\|<\epsilon\)
  • contraction: \(P:x\rightarrow x\) is a contraction if \(\exists 0 \le C <1\) s.t. \(\forall x,y \in X,\|P(x)-P(y)\|\le C\|x-y\|\)
  • fixed point: \(x^* = P(x^*)\)

2 - finite escape time
\(\Sigma:\dot x = f(x),x(t_0)=x_0\)
\(\Sigma\) has a finite escape time if \(\exists T<\infty\) s.t. \(x(t,x_0)\rightarrow \infty,t\rightarrow T\).
can we express this as a solution?

  • if f is p.w. in time or f is locally Lipschitz \(\implies\) \(x(t,x_0)\) exists & is unique \(\forall t\in [t_0,t_0+\delta]\).
  • if f is globally Lipschitz, \(\implies \forall T>t_0,x(t,x_0)\) exists & is unique \(\forall t\in [t_0,T]\implies\) no finite escape time.
    example: \(\dot x = -x^2,x(0)=-1\)
  • not globally Lipschitz \(\rightarrow\) cannot determine if there is a finite escape time
  • \(\frac{1}{t-1}\rightarrow \infty\) as \(t\rightarrow 1\) shows finite escape time
    example: \(\dot x = -x^2,x(0)=1\)
  • no finite escape time
    \(\dot x = x,x(0)=-1\)
  • globally lipschitz so no finite escape time
  • only reaches infinity when t is at infinity

3- gronwall-bellman inequality

  • \(\dot y (t)=\mu(t)y(t),y(a)=\lambda\iff y(t)=\lambda+\int^t_a \mu(\tau)y(\tau)d\tau\)
  • \(\iff y(t)=\lambda \exp[\int^t_a\mu(\tau)d\tau]\)
  • if \(y(t)\le\lambda+\int^t_a \mu(\tau)y(\tau)d\tau\), GB inequality asks if this implies \(y(t)\le\lambda \exp[\int^t_a\mu(\tau)d\tau]\)
  • assumes \(\lambda\) is constant and \(\mu\) is continuous & non-negative
  • implicit inequality vs. explicit inequality
  • proof in class
  • why is this useful? we use it for continuous dependence on IC

4 - continuous dependence on IC

  • \(\dot x= f(t,x),x(t_0)=x_0\)
  • \(\Psi: \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow C_n[t_0,t_0+\delta]\)
  • we have continuous dependence on IC \(\iff \Psi\) is continuous at \(x_0\)
  • \(\iff \forall \varepsilon >0 \exists \delta >0\) s.t. \(\forall z_0\in B_\delta(x_0)\implies\exists \Psi(z_0) \in B_\epsilon(\Psi(x_0))\)
  • \(\iff\|x_0-z_0\|_2<\delta \implies \|\Psi(x_0)-\Psi(z_0)\|_\infty<\epsilon\)
  • f is p.w. cont. in time, f is globally lipschitz, \(\implies\) continuous dependence on IC
  • proof:
    \[\begin{align*} & \dot x = f(t,x),x(t_0)=x_0,x(t)=x_0+\int f(\tau)x(\tau)d\tau \\ & \dot z = f(t,z),z(t_0)=z_0,z(t)=z_0+\int f(\tau)z(\tau)d\tau \\ & \Psi(x_0)-\Psi(z_0)=x_0-z_0 +\int f(\tau)x(\tau)-f(\tau)z(\tau)d\tau \\ & \|\Psi(x_0)-\Psi(z_0)\|\le\|x_0-z_0\| +\int \|f(\tau)x(\tau)-f(\tau)z(\tau)\|d\tau \\ & y(t)=\|x(t)-z(t)\| \le \|x_0-z_0\| +\int L\|x(\tau)-z(\tau)\|d\tau \\ & \|x(t)-z(t)\| \le \lambda + \int \mu(\tau)\|x(\tau)-z(\tau)\|d\tau \\ & \|x(t)-z(t)\| \le \|x_0-z_0\|\exp[\int L_T dt] \\ \end{align*} \]
    • for a given \(\epsilon\), find \(\delta\) s.t. the above holds (look at this later)

5 - Lasalle's invariance theorem

  • let \(x_e=0\) be an eq. point of \(\dot x= f(x)\). let d be an open set containing \(x_e\). let \(V:D\rightarrow \mathbb{R}\) be \(C^1\)
    example: \(\dot x_1 = x_2, \dot x_2=-x_1^3-x_2^3\)
  • eq point at (0,0)
  • indirect method:
    \[\begin{align*} & A=\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\implies \text{ no conclusion} \\ & V(x)=1/4x_1^2+1/2x_2^2 \\ & \dot V(x) = x_1^3x_2+x_2[-x_1^3-x_2^3] \\ & = -x_2^4 \le 0\implies \text{SISL}\\ \end{align*} \]
    • from here, look at Lasalle's invariance
    • find \(S=\{x|\dot V(x)=0\}=\{x_2=0\}\)
    • from here, we get \(\dot x_1=0,\dot x_2=0,\implies x_1=0\)
    • and radially unbounded
