theorem: \(\dot x = f(x)\) - f is locally Lipschitz

  • \(x_0 \in W\) is a compact set & all solutions \(x(t,x_0)\in W\) then unique solution exists \(\forall t \ge 0\).

lyapunov stability theory:
\(\dot x = f(x)\) (time-invariant)

  • definition: let \(x_e\) be an equilibrium point s.t. \(\exists \rho>0\) for which \(x_0 \in B_\rho (x_e)\implies x(t,x_0)\) exists & is unique \(\forall t \ge 0\).

(a) \(x_e\) is stable in the sense of lyapunov (SISL) if \(\forall \varepsilon > 0,\space \exists \delta>0\) s.t. \(x_0 \in B_\delta(x_e) \implies x(t,x_0) \in B_\varepsilon(x_e)\)
(b) \(x_e\) is unstable otherwise
(c) \(x_e\) is asymptotically stable (AS) if it is SISL and \(\exists \eta >0\) s.t. \(x_0 \in B_\eta(x_e) \implies \lim_{t\rightarrow \infty}||x(t,x_0)-x_e||=0\)

  • AS = SISL + attractivity

recall: \(\Psi: \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{C}_n[0,\infty)\)

  • \(x_e\) is SISL \(\iff \Psi\) is continuous at \(x_e\).

theorem: Lyapunov stability theorem (lyapunov direct method):
assume \(x_e=0\) is an eq. pt of \(\dot x = f(x), \space \exists D \subset \mathbb{R}^n\) open set about \(x_e=0\) s.t.
(1) \(f:D\rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n\) locally Lipschitz
(2) \(\exists V:D \rightarrow \mathbb{R}, \space V \in \mathbb{C}^1\) s.t.
(a) \(V(0)=0\)
(b) \(V(x)>0,x\in D\) \ \([0]\)
(c) \(\dot V(x) \le 0, \forall x \in D\)
then \(x_e\) is SISL
moreover, if \(\dot V(x)<0,\forall x \in D\) \ \([0]\), then \(x_e\) is AS

proof: part 1 \(\rightarrow\) SISL

(1) \(\exists \rho > 0\) s.t. \(x_0 \in B_\rho(0) \implies\) solution exists on \([0,\infty)\) & is unique
(2) \(\forall \varepsilon > 0 ,\exists \delta>0\) s.t. \(x_0 \in B_\delta(0) \implies x(t,x_0)\in B_\varepsilon(0)\)

D is open in \(\mathbb{R}^n \implies\) can choose \(\varepsilon\) s.t. \(B_\varepsilon (0) \subset D\)

  • choose \(0 < r < \varepsilon\) s.t. \(B_r(0) \subset B_\varepsilon (0)\)
  • \(\bar B_r := [x \in D \text{ s.t. } ||x|| \le r]\)
    define \(\alpha=\min_{||x||=r}V(x)>0\forall x \not = 0\) (V is continuous & \(V(x)>0\forall x \not = 0\))
  • fix \(\beta \in (0,\infty)\) & define \(\Omega_\beta=[x \in \bar B_r (0) | V(x) \le \beta]\)
  • by construction \(\Omega_\beta \subset \bar B_r(0)\) (open ball)
  • claim 0: \(\Omega_\beta \subset \bar B_r(0)\)
    • proof: if \(\exists x \in \Omega_\beta\) s.t. \(||x|| = r\) then \(V(x) \ge \alpha\)
  • claim 1: solutions that start in \(\Omega_\beta\) stay in \(\Omega_\beta\)
    • proof: \(\forall x_0 \in \Omega_\beta,\) \(\Omega_\beta\) compact \(\implies\) solution exists + is unique \(t\in[0,\infty)\)
    • fix \(\phi (t)\) solution of \(\dot x = f(x)\) defined on \([t_1,t_2],\phi(t_1)\in \Omega_\beta\)
    • \(\phi(t_1)\in \Omega_\beta \iff V(\phi(t_1)) \le \beta \implies V(\phi(t)) \le \beta \forall t \implies \phi(t) \in \Omega_\beta \forall t\)
  • claim 2: \(\exists \delta > 0\) s.t. \(B_\delta \subset \Omega_\beta\)
    • proof: V is continuous at 0 \(\implies||x-0|| < \delta \implies |V(x)-V(0)|<\beta\)

thus \(x_0 \in B_\delta(0) \implies x_0 \in \Omega_\beta\) (claim 2)

  • \(\implies x(t,x_0) \in \Omega_\beta\) and \(x(t,x_0)\) exists + is unique \(\forall t \ge 0\) (claim 1)
  • \(\implies x(t,x_0)\in B_\varepsilon(0) \implies\) SISL

part 2 (AS): \(\dot V(x) < 0\) \(\forall x \in D\) \ {0}
proof:

  • claim 3: \(||x_0||<\delta \implies \lim_{t \rightarrow \infty}||x(t,\infty)||=0\)
    • proof: \(||x_0||<\delta \implies x(t,x_0) \in \Omega_\beta \forall t \ge 0\)
    • we have \(\dot V(x(t,x_0)) \le 0 \forall t \ge 0 \implies V(x(t,x_0))\) is not increasing
    • \(\implies \lim_{t\rightarrow \infty}V(x(t,x_0))=C\) to show \(C=0\)
  • to show C =0 suppose \(C \not = 0\) then \(V(x(t,x_0)) \ge C/2 \forall t \ge 0\)
    • \(\implies x(t,x_0)\) never enters \(\Omega_{C/2}\)
    • since V is continuous, \(\exists d > 0\) s.t. \(B_d(0) \subset \Omega_{C/2}\)
    • \(\therefore x(t,x)\) lies in annulus: \(-\gamma :=\max_{d \le ||x|| \le r}\dot V < 0\)
    • \(V(x(t,x_0)) = V(x_0)+\int^t_0\dot V(x(\tau,x_0))d\tau\)
      • = \(V(x_0)+\int^t_0-\gamma d\tau=v(x_0)-\gamma(t-t_0)\)
        • where \(\lim_{t\rightarrow \infty} = -\infty\) (contradiction)
