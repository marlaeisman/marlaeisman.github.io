continuity

definition: let \((v,||\cdot || )\&(w,|||\cdot|||)\) be normed spaces. A function h: V \(\rightarrow\) W is continuous at \(x_0 \in V\) if \(\forall \varepsilon > 0,\exists \delta_{\varepsilon}>0\) s.t. \(||x-x_0||<\delta \implies |||h(x)-h(x_0)|||<\varepsilon \iff x\in B_\delta (x_0) \implies h(x) \in B_\varepsilon(h(x_0))\)

  • every ball of radius \(\delta\) in V gets mapped to a ball of radius \(\varepsilon\) in W.
    example:
  • how do we show that a function is not continuous?
    • for any \(\varepsilon < 1, \not \exists \delta\)
    • h is continuous if it is continuous \(\forall x \in V\).

what about a stronger notion of continuity?

  • Lipschitz continuity: if we join any 2 points along the function, the slope should be less than the Lipschitz constant
  • (at a particular point) - h: V \(\rightarrow\) W is Lipschitz continuous at \(x_0 \in V\) if \(\exists r > 0,L<\infty,|||h(x)-h(y)|||\le L||x-y||\)
    • where L = Lipschitz constant
  • h is locally Lipschitz if h is Lipschitz continuous \(\forall x_0 \in V\).
  • h is globally Lipschitz continuous if (in addition to above), \(B_r (x_0)=V\).
    • \(h(x)=x^2\) is locally but not globally Lipschitz
  • remarks:
    • Lipschitz cont. at \(x_0 \implies\) cont. at \(x_0\).
      • stronger notion of continuity
    • suppose h is differentiable
      • h:\(R^n\rightarrow R^n \& \exists r>0,L<\infty,x_0\in R^n\) s.t. \(\forall x \in B_r(x_0)||\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}(x_0)||\le L\) then h is Lipschitz cont. at \(x_0\).
      • look at the derivative at every point... if it is bounded by L \(\implies\) cont.
    • If \(h: V \rightarrow V\) is Lipschitz w.r.t. \(||\cdot||, \& |||\cdot||| \equiv||\cdot ||\implies\) h is Lipschitz w.r.t. \(|||\cdot|||\).
      • but L could be different

example: h: R\(\rightarrow\)R
\[h(x):= \text{sat}(x) := \begin{matrix} x, & |x|<1 \\ 1, & x \ge 1 \\ -1, & x \le -1 \end{matrix}\]

  • something like a sigmoid is L.C.

example: piecewise continuity

  • requires jumps to be finite (not \(\tan(x)\))
  • def: let h: R \(\rightarrow\) V. h is piecewise (pw) cont. if
    a) \(\forall k > 0\in Z,h:[-k,k]\rightarrow V\) is cont. except at a finite number of points
    b) a point of discontinuity may have \(\lim_{\delta \rightarrow 0}h(t_i,+\delta) \& \lim_{\delta \rightarrow 0}h(t_0,+\delta)\) both exist & are finite.

example:
\[f(x) = \begin{matrix} 1, & x \text{ is rational} \\ 0, & x \text{ is irrational} \end{matrix}\]
is not piecewise continuous because it does not satisfy a.

solutions of differential equations in \(R^n\)
\(\Sigma : \dot x \ f(t,x),t\ge t_0; x(t_0)=x_0\)

  • def: \(\phi(t)\) is a soln. of \(\Sigma\) on \([t_0,t_1]\) if
    • a) \(\dot \phi(t)\exists\) on the interval \([t_0,t_1]\)
    • b) \(\dot \phi(t)=f(t, \phi(t)) \forall t \in [t_0,t_1]\)
    • c)\(\phi(t_0)=x_0\)
  • notation: \(x(t)=\phi(t,t_0, x_0)=\phi_t(t_0,x_0)\)

equivalent integral definition:
\[\begin{align*} \dot x = f(t,x(t)),x(t_0)=x_0\iff \int^t_{t_0}\dot x(\tau)d\tau=\int^t_{t_0}(f(\tau), x(\tau))d\tau \\ \iff x(\tau)|^t_{t_0}=\int^t_{t_0}(f(\tau), x(\tau))d\tau \\ \iff x(t) = x_0 + \int^t_{t_0}(f(\tau), x(\tau))d\tau \end{align*}\]

  • def: \(\phi(t)\) is soln. of \(\Sigma\) on \([t_0,t_1]\) if \(\forall \bar t \in [t_0,t_1],\bar t\)
    • \(\phi(\bar t) =x_0 + \int^{\bar t}_{t_0}(f(\tau), x(\tau))d\tau\)

theorem (3.1 khalil): local existence of uniqueness

  • given (a) \(\dot x = f(t,x(t)),\forall t \ge t_0 ; x(t_0)=x_0\)
  • and (b) f is p.w. cont. in t
  • and (c) \(\exists T>t_0,r>0,L<\infty\) s.t. \(\forall x,y\in B_r(x_0) \& t\in [t_0,T]\)
    • \(||f(t,x)-f(t,y)||\le L||x-y||\)
    • (f is L.C. w.r.t. x)
  • then: \(\exists \delta>0\) s.t. soln exists & is unique on \(t_0,t_0+\delta\) !!!

proof: \(\delta > 0\) to be found. key idea is contraction mapping.

  • for contraction mapping we need banach space and a function on the space that is a contraction.
  • \(C_n [t_0,t_0+\delta]=(\phi:[t_0,t_0+\delta]\rightarrow R^n|\phi\) continuous). is a complete space
  • \(||\phi||_\infty = \sup_{t_0\le t \le t_0+\delta} ||\phi(t)||\)
  • define P: \(C_n[t_0,t_0+\delta]\rightarrow C_n[t_0,t_0+\delta]\)
    • this maps \(\phi \rightarrow P(\phi) \implies \phi(t) \rightarrow P(\phi)(t)\)
  • define \(P(\phi)(t) :=x_0 + \int^t_{t_0}(f(\tau), \phi(\tau))d\tau\)
    • we now need to show that P is a contraction, and then apply C.M. theorem to show that x is a fixed point.
  • if x(t) is a fixed point of P, then x(t)=P(x(t))
    • \(x(t)=x_0 + \int^t_{t_0}(f(\tau), \phi(\tau))d\tau\)
    • if this is satisfied + unique, we can solve the DE
    • we only need to show that P is a contraction
  • when is P is a contraction?????
    • let \(x(t),y(t) \in C_n[t_0,t_0+\delta]\)
    • \(||P(x)=P(y)||_\infty \le C||x-y||_\infty\) to show \(0\le C < 1\)
    • to show: for \(C<1,\sup_{t_0\le t \le t_0+\delta} ||P(x)(t)-P(y)(t)||\le C \sup_{t_0\le t \le t_0+\delta} ||x(t)-y(t)||\)
    • fix t: \(P(x)(t)-P(y)(t)=\int_{t_0}^t f(\tau,x(\tau))-f(\tau, y(\tau))d \tau\)
    • \(||P(x)(t)=P(y)(t)||=\int_{t_0}^t f(\tau,x(\tau))-f(\tau, y(\tau))d \tau||\)
    • \(\le \int_{t_0}^t f(\tau,x(\tau))-f(\tau, y(\tau))d \tau||\) (triangle inequality)
    • \(\le \int_{t_0}^t ||x(\tau)-y(\tau)||d \tau\)
    • \(\le \int_{t_0}^t ||x-y||_\infty d \tau=L(t-t_0)||x-y||_\infty\)
    • \(\implies \sup_{t_0\le t \le t_0+\delta}||P(x)(t)=P(y)(t)||\le \sup_{t_0\le t \le t_0+\delta} L(t-t_0) ||x-y||_\infty\)
    • = \(L\delta ||x-y||_\infty\)
    • need \(L \delta < 1\)
      • pick \(\delta = 1/L\)
      • this implies existence of unique solution
