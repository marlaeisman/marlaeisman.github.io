these definitions relate to existence and uniqueness of DEs

convergence of sequences:

  • a seq (\(x_n\)) in (v,||\(\cdot\)||) converges to a point \(\bar x\) if \(\forall \varepsilon \geq 0, \exists N< \infty s.t. \forall k \geq N, x_k \in B_\varepsilon (\bar x)\)
    • \(\implies ||x_k - \bar x|| < \varepsilon\)
      cauchy sequence:
  • a seq. (\(x_n\)) is cauchy if \(\forall \varepsilon > 0, \exists N < \infty, \forall n, m \geq N, ||x_n-x_m||<\varepsilon\)
  • fact: if \(x_n \rightarrow \bar x\) as \(n \rightarrow \infty\), then \((x_n)\) is cauchy.
  • proof: let \(\varepsilon > 0\), since \(x_n \rightarrow \bar x,\exists N<\infty\) s.t. \(\forall n \geq N, ||x_n-\bar x||<\varepsilon/2\)
    • let n,m \(\geq\) N, \(||x_n-x_m||=||(\bar x_n - \bar x)-(x_m - \bar x)||\) (triangle ineq.)
    • =\(\leq||(x_n - \bar x)+(\bar x - x_m )||< \varepsilon/2 + \varepsilon/2 = \varepsilon\)
  • def: (V, ||\(\cdot\)||) is complete if every cauchy sequence converges to a point in V
  • facts:
    • a) every finite dim. vector space is complete
    • b) (\(\subset[a,b],||\cdot||_\infty\)) (continuous space) is complete
  • def: a complete normed space is called a banach space.

contraction mapping:

  • let (x,|\(|\cdot|\)|) be a banach (complete norm space) & let P\(:x\rightarrow x\)
    • def: a point \(x^* \in x\) is a fixed point (of P) if \(P(x^*)=x^*\)
    • def: P is a contraction if \(\exists\) a const. \(0\leq C <1\) s.t. \(\forall x,y \in x,||P(x),P(y)\leq C||x-y||\)
    • do they map closer together?
  • when is there 1 fixed point? when is there exactly 1?

contraction mapping principle:

  • let (x,\(||\cdot||\)) be a complete normed space & let P:\(x\rightarrow x\) be a contraction. then, \(\exists ! x^* \in X\) s.t. \(P(x^* = x^*)\).
  • moreover, \(\forall x_0 \in X\), define seq. \(x_{n+1}=P(x_n),n \geq 0\). Then \(x_n\) is cauchy & converges to \(x^*\).
    remarks:
  • a) uniqueness: suppose P(\(x^*\))=\(x^*\), P(\(y^*\))=\(y^*\) (2 different fixed points).
  • ||\(x^*-y^*\)||=||P(\(x^*\))-P(\(y^*\)) \(\geq C ||x^*-y^*||,0\leq C < 1 \implies ||x^*-y^*||=0 \implies x^*-y^*\)
  • why cauchy property holds:
  • \(||x_{n+1}-x_n||=||P(x_n)-P(x_{n-1})||\leq C ||x_n-x_{n-1}=C||P(x_n)-P(x_{n-1})||\)...\(\leq C^n||x_1-x_0||\)
    • \(\lim_{n\rightarrow \infty}C^n||x_1-x_0||=0\)

example:

  • (\(R^2,||\cdot||_2\)) is complete because it is a finite directional vector space
  • find solution of Ax=b, \(x \in R^2\) by contraction mapping principle
  • define \(P(x)=x+(Ax-b)\)
    • note: \(P(x^*)=x^* \iff x^* +Ax^*-b=x^*\iff Ax^*-b=0\iff Ax^*=b\)
    • where \(x^*\) is the solution of Ax=b
  • when is P a contraction?
    • \(||P(x)-P(y)||=||x+(Ax-b)-y-(Ay-b)||\)
      • =\(||(I+A)x-b-((I+A)y-b)||=||(I+A)(x-y)||\)
        • *norm of matrix * vector
        • \(\leq ||I+A||_i||x-y||=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(I+A)^{\top}(I+A)}||x-y||\)
      • P is a contraction when \(||I+A||_i=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(I+A)^{\top}(I+A)}||<1\)
      • note \(||Ax||\leq ||A||_i ||x|| \sqrt{\lambda_\max A^\top A}\)

for i=1:50
x(:,i+1)=x():,i)+(Ax(:,i)-b); \(\rightarrow\) converges to solution of Ax=b

let sequence converge to \(x^*\) to show \(P(x^*)=x^*=P(\lim x_n)=\lim P(x_n)=\lim x_{n+1}=x^*\)

continuous functions:

  • let \((V,||\cdot||) \& (W, ||\cdot||)\) be normed spaces. a function \(h: V \rightarrow W\) is continuous at \(x_0 \in V\)
  • \(\forall \varepsilon > 0, \exists \delta > 0\) s.t. \(||x-x_0||<\delta \implies ||h(x)-h(x_0)||<\varepsilon\)
    • continuous at a particular point
  • h is continuous throughout the space
    • if \(\bar h\) is continuous \(\forall x_0 \in V\)
