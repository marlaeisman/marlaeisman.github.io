\(\ddot y = -ky\)
\(x_1=y,x_2=\dot y\)
\(\implies \dot x_1 = x_2, \dot x_2 = -kx_1\)

note how system changes between k=1 (SISL) and k=-1 (unstable).
step 1: construct sliding surface
\(s=ax_1+x_2\)

dynamics on \(s=0\):
\(x_2=-ax_1\)
\(\dot x_1 = x_2=-ax_1\)
\(x_1 \rightarrow 0\) as \(t \rightarrow \infty\)
\(\implies x_2 \rightarrow 0\)

how fast do we reach the origin? finite time
remember for dynamics, we choose \(k=\text{sign}(sx_1)\)

more general example:
\(\dot x_1 = x_2\)
\(\dot x_2 = h(x)+g(x)u\)
\(h(x),g(x)\) may be unknown but satisfy conditions: \(g(x)>g_0>0,\forall x \in \mathbb{R}^2\)

i want to design some state feedback \(u(x)\) s.t. \(x=[0,0]\) is a.s.

  • construct a sliding surface, look at the dynamics on the sliding surface to ensure they are a.s. and the dynamics off the sliding surface to ensure they are attractive. if we have both then we can guarantee a.s.
  1. construct sliding surface. what would the surface be?
    \(s=ax_1+x_2,a>0\)
  2. look at dynamics ON the surface
    on \(s=0, x_2=-ax_1\)
    \(\dot x_1=x_2=ax_1\implies\) a.s. \(\implies x_1\rightarrow 0, x_2\rightarrow 0\) as \(t \rightarrow \infty\)
    note: dynamics on s are independent of h(x) and g(x)
  3. look at reaching mode
    look at dynamics off \(s=0\implies s\not = 0\)
    \(\dot s =a \dot x_1 + \dot x_2\)
    here, we only know that \(\dot s = ax_2+h(x)+g(x)u\)

suppose \(h(x),g(x)\) satisfy \(|\frac{ax_2+h(x)}{g(x)}|\le \sigma(x),\forall x \in \mathbb{R}^2\implies \sigma(x)>0\)

  • here, we just need to know \(\sigma(x)\)
  • to find u to drive \(s\) to zero, we want to first find a lyapunov function

\(V(x)=\frac{1}{2}s^2\)
\(\dot V=s \dot s = s[ax_2+h(x)]+sg(x)u\)
\(\dot V = sg(x)\frac{ax_2+h(x)}{g(x)}+sg(x)u\)
\(\le g(x) |s| |\frac{ax_2+h(x)}{g(x)}|+sg(x)u\)
select \(u=-\beta(x)\text{sign}(s)\)
\(\beta(x)\ge \sigma(x) + \beta_0,\beta_0>0\)

\(\dot V \le g(x) |s| \sigma(x)-g(x)[\sigma(x)+\beta_0]s \cdot \text{sign}(s)\)
\(=g(x)\{\sigma(x)-\sigma(x)+\beta_0\}\)
\(\le -g_0 \beta_0 |s| < 0 \implies V \rightarrow 0 \implies s=0\)
\(=-g_0\beta_0cV^{1/2}\) where \(c=\sqrt 2\)
this shows that we can drive V to zero in finite time
control is robust to \(h(x),g(x)\) (see hw 7)

suppose we have a 3-state system

\(\dot x_0=x_1\)
\(\dot x_1=x_2\)
\(\dot x_2=h(x)+g(x)u\)
\(x = [x_1 \space x_2 \space x_3]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^3\)

how do we construct a sliding surface? (hw 7)

first make the system of \(\dot x_1\) and \(\dot x_0\) stable + go to the origin

\[\begin{bmatrix} \dot x_0 \\ \dot x_1 \end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -a_0 & -a_1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x_0 \\ x_1 \end{bmatrix}\]
\(x_2 = -a_0x_0-a_1x_1,a_0>0,a_1>0\)
\(s=a_0x_0+a_1x_1+x_2\)
(C1) \(g(x)>g_0>0\)
(C2) \(|\frac{a_0x_1+a_1x_2+h(x)}{g(x)}|\)
