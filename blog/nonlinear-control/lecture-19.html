recall definitions of FB linearizable, relative degree:

in linearizing control: \(u=L_gL_f^{r-1}h(x)^{-1}\{-L_f^rh(x)+v\}\implies y^r=v\)
zero dynamics: dynamics restricted to Z
\(z = q(0,z)\)
theorem: if \(z=0\) is locally exponentially stable for the zero dynamics \(z=q(0,z)\), then \(u_{IO},v=-k\zeta\) locally exponentially stabilizes \(z=0\)

proof: \(\zeta = A_{cl} \zeta, A_{cl}=A-Bk, z=q(\zeta, z)\)

\[A_{cl}=\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & \dots \\ \vdots & \ddots \\ & & 0 & 1 \\ -k_1 & -k_2 & ... & -k_r \end{bmatrix}, Re(\lambda_i(A_{cl}))<0 \]
linearize about \(\zeta\), z=0
\[\begin{bmatrix} \dot \delta \zeta \\ \dot \delta z \end{bmatrix} = J\begin{bmatrix} \delta \zeta \\ \delta z \end{bmatrix} \]
\[J=\begin{bmatrix} A_{CL} & 0 \\ \frac{\partial q(0,0)}{\partial \zeta} & \frac{\partial q(0,0) }{\partial z} \end{bmatrix}, \frac{\partial q(0,0) }{\partial z} \]
J and \(A_{cl}\) are hurwitz \(\implies\) x is stable exponentially

IO linearization vs FB linearization
suppose relative degree r=n and define \(u=L_gL_f^{r-1}h(x)^{-1}(-L_f^nh(x)+v)\)
then, \[\zeta = \begin{bmatrix} h(x) \\ L_fh(x) \\ \vdots \\ L_f^{r-1}h(x) \end{bmatrix}, \dot \zeta = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & \dots \\ & & \ddots \\ \end{bmatrix}\zeta + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}v \]
\(\dot x = f(x) + g(z) u, y=h(x)\)
when \(u=u_{IO}\) and \(\zeta = T(x)\rightarrow\) \(\dot \zeta = A \zeta Bv\)
so now we want to find conditions s.t. \(T(x)\) is a diffeomorphism

inverse function theorem:
if a function \(\tau:\mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n, T \in C^1, \frac{\partial \tau}{\partial x}(x_0)\) is full rank, then \(T^-1\) exists and is continually differential locally

fact:
if nonlinear function \(\Sigma\) has relative degree r at \(x_0\), then in a nbd @ \(x_0\), the functions \(\{h(x),L_fh(x), L_f^2h(x), ...L_f^{r-1}h(x)\}\) are independent

therefore, \(\Sigma_{NL}\) is FB linearizable iff \(\exists h(x)\) s.t. relative degree r=n.

example:
\(\dot x = f(x) + g(x)u \rightarrow\) is it FB linearizable?

  • start by trying y
  • but can we know when such an h exists? this depends only on \(f(x), g(x)\).

prereq: (i) Lie bracket: manifold, tangent space, tangent vector, vector, field
(ii) involute distribution: tangent bundle, distributions

manifold:

  • surface in \(\mathbb{R}^n\), maybe with orientation of plane, sphere, etc.
  • have a dimension i.e. # to specify points on manifold

    definition: let M be a non-empty subset of \(\mathbb{R}^n\) and let the dimension of M be \(1 \le m \le n\), then M is an m-dim smooth manifold of \(\mathbb{R}^n\) if \(\forall p \in M, \exists r>0, F=B_r(p)\rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{n-m}\) s.t.
    (a) \(M \cap B_r(p)=\{x \in \mathbb{R}^n | F(x)=0\}\)
    (b) F is smooth
    (c) \(\forall \bar x \in M \cap B_r(p),\) rank \(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}(\bar x)=n-m\)
    \(\implies\) F is diffomorphism

unit spheres:
\(x \in \mathbb{R}^2 \rightarrow F(x)=x_1^2+x_2^2-1\)
\(\mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow F(c) = x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2 -1\)
unit circle in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)
\[F(x) = \begin{bmatrix} x_1^2+x_2^2-1 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} \]
tangent space:
linear approximation of a manifold M @ point \(p \in M\)

definition:
let M be a smooth manifold on \(\mathbb{R}^n, P \in M, F: B_r(p) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{n-m}\). then, the tangent space @ p, \(\tau_p M = \{v \in \mathbb{R}^n | \frac{\partial F}{\partial x}(p)v=0\}=\text{Null}(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x})\)
\(\dim(\tau_p M)= \dim(M)=m\)
